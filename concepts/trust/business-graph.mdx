The **business graph** is what a SOLO network can hold about a legal entity: a
core identity, plus the verification events participants have furnished about
it. Each verification branch is a candidate
[trust asset](/concepts/trust/trust-assets) — an attested verification that an
authorized participant can build on instead of repeating registry lookups,
ownership tracing, and risk screening.

This page shows the main branches that exist **today**. Every node is a real
data model; branches with no backing model are deliberately absent rather than
implied.

## The graph

Node names are the model names used in consent grants (`models`).
`GET /v1/graph/models` lists the same models — for example `BusinessIdentity`,
with the resource path `business-identities` — together with every field.

```text

│   business_legal_name, business_dba_name,
│   business_jurisdiction_of_formation,
│   business_registration_identifier_from_jurisdiction_of_formation,

│   business_registration_status, business_website_url, business_email,
│   business_phone, employee_count
│
├── address_identity                                type, line_1, line_2, city, state, zip, country
│
├── business_identity_verification_event            is_identity_verified, registration_status,
│                                                   registration_identifier, jurisdiction_of_formation,
│                                                   operational_existence_status, tax_id_validation_result,
│                                                   address_verification_result, is_secretary_of_state_match,
│                                                   formation_document_artifact
│
├── business_ownership_control_verification_event   is_ownership_control_verified,
│                                                   beneficial_owners_identified_count,
│                                                   control_persons_identified_count,
│                                                   authorized_representatives_identified_count,
│                                                   beneficial_ownership_determination_method,
```

## How to read the graph

- **`business_identity`** is the [entity](/concepts/identity/entities) itself.
  Its federal EIN and jurisdiction of formation together are the match key
  that lets contributions from different participants attach to one business.
- **Verification events** are furnished outcomes. A
  [KYB certificate](/api-overview/querying/kyb-certificate-snapshot) query
  consolidates the three verification branches — identity, ownership and
  control, and risk and compliance — into one certificate.
- **Addresses** are `address_identity` records attached to the business. The
  KYB certificate reports the registered and operating addresses inside its
  business identity verification sub-product.
- **`business_consent_event`** is the permission record that governs querying
  the business.

## Each branch carries provenance

Furnished verification events record the participant that furnished them, and
can link to that participant's attestation. Every populated KYB sub-product
surfaces this as `furnishing_entity_id` and `attestation_event_id`, plus an
`assertions` block recording what was asserted and when. The identity and
ownership branches also carry evidence artifact fields
(`formation_document_artifact`, `ownership_evidence_artifact`). See
[Provenance](/concepts/trust/provenance) for the full lineage model.

<Note>
  What you can actually read back is decided by the network's
  [querying policy](/concepts/governance/querying-policies) and by
  [entitlement](/concepts/governance/entitlement). The graph describes what
  *can* exist; consent, policy, and entitlement decide what *you* see.
</Note>

## Trust assets for a business

Consolidated through products, the business graph yields assets such as:

- **Verified business identity** — registration, jurisdiction, and tax-ID
  validation from a [KYB certificate](/api-overview/querying/kyb-certificate-snapshot).
- **Verified ownership and control** — beneficial owners and control persons,
  with the evidence reviewed.
- **Risk and compliance screening** — sanctions screening, adverse media, and
  activity-risk outcomes.
