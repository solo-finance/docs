The **consumer graph** is what a SOLO network can hold about a person: a core
identity, plus the records and verification events participants have furnished
about them. Each verification branch is a candidate
[trust asset](/concepts/trust/trust-assets) — an attested verification that an
authorized participant can build on instead of repeating.

This page shows the main branches that exist **today**. Every node is a real
data model; branches with no backing model are deliberately absent rather than
implied.

## The graph

Node names are the model names used in consent grants (`models`).
`GET /v1/graph/models` lists the same models — for example `ConsumerIdentity`,
with the resource path `consumer-identities` — together with every field.

```text
consumer_identity                         The person
│   first_name, last_name, date_of_birth, personal_email, social_security_number
│
├── email_identity                        email_address
├── phone_number_identity                 phone_number
│   └── phone_number_verification_event   phone_verified, phone_carrier_name,
│                                         phone_age_days, phone_risk_score
├── address_identity                      line_1, line_2, city, state, zip, country
│   └── address_capture_event             is_address_verified, address_verification_outcome,
│                                         address_match_quality, address_verification_method_type
│
├── identity_document_identity            type, number, issuing_state, issuing_country,
│                                         expiration_date, upload
├── document_capture_event                is_document_captured, document_type,
│                                         document_capture_method, is_document_attribute_reviewed
├── biometric_capture_event               is_biometric_captured, biometric_capture_method,
│                                         biometric_comparison_outcome
├── liveness_check_event                  is_liveness_captured, check_result, capture_method_type
├── identity_corroboration_event          is_ssn_match, is_name_match, is_dob_match,
│                                         identity_corroboration_outcome, kyc_decision
│
├── income_verification_event             is_income_verified, base_salary, monthly_income,
│                                         debt_to_income_ratio, paystub_gross_pay_amount
├── employment_verification_event         is_employment_verified, employer_name, job_title,
│                                         employment_status, length_of_employment_months
│
├── fraud_verification_event              confirmed_fraud_indicator, fraud_attribute_label,
│                                         fraud_loss_event_category, fraud_event_date
├── bad_actor_event                       bad_actor_list_placement_date, bad_actor_reason_code
├── financial_crimes_watch_event          event_category, event_date, signal_level
│
└── consumer_consent_event                scope, expires_at, consented_fields
```

## How to read the graph

- **`consumer_identity`** is the [entity](/concepts/identity/entities) itself.
  Its SSN and date of birth together are the match key that lets
  contributions from different participants attach to one person.
- **Identity records** — email, phone, address, identity document — are
  attached to the person, and some carry their own verification events (a
  phone number's verification, an address's capture and verification).
- **Verification events** are furnished outcomes. The KYC events — document,
  biometric, liveness, address, and identity corroboration — are what a
  [KYC certificate](/api-overview/querying/kyc-certificate-snapshot) query
  consolidates into up to nine sub-products. Income, employment, and fraud
  verification are separate models that can also be furnished.
- **Screening events** — `bad_actor_event` and `financial_crimes_watch_event`
  — back the [screening list](/api-overview/querying/screening-lists)
  products.
- **`consumer_consent_event`** is not data *about* the consumer so much as the
  permission record that governs querying them.

## Each branch carries provenance

A verification event becomes a reusable trust asset because its lineage travels
with it. Furnished events record the participant that furnished them, and can
link to that participant's attestation. Certificate sub-products surface this as
`furnishing_entity_id` and `attestation_event_id`, plus an `assertions` block
recording what was asserted and when. Some records also carry a reference to
the evidence behind them, such as an identity document's `upload`. See
[Provenance](/concepts/trust/provenance) for the full lineage model.

<Note>
  What you can actually read back is decided by the network's
  [querying policy](/concepts/governance/querying-policies) and by
  [entitlement](/concepts/governance/entitlement). The graph describes what
  *can* exist; consent, policy, and entitlement decide what *you* see.
</Note>

## Trust assets for a consumer

Consolidated through products, the consumer graph yields assets such as:

- **Verified identity** — a [KYC certificate](/api-overview/querying/kyc-certificate-snapshot)
  consolidating document, biometric, liveness, address, and corroboration
  evidence.
- **Verified address** — address capture and verification outcomes.
- **Screening** — [screening list](/api-overview/querying/screening-lists)
  results drawn from furnished bad-actor and financial-crimes watch events.
